All of the tipping was cash. None of it was card. Cash buyers paid 500.00 for 360.00 of food. That is the single most striking number in this report and it has nothing to do with cost control.
29.70 of sales tax was not collected on the 24 cash plates. Card plates rang at 16.24 with tax; cash plates went out at 15.00 flat. Texas generally holds the seller liable for the tax on a taxable sale whether or not it was collected at the counter, which would make this 29.70 payable out of the 443.38 above, bringing it to 413.68. Whether it is owed at all turns on entity status, and Ghetto Kitchen LLC is an LLC rather than a qualified nonprofit, so the tax-free sale day provisions that apply to charitable organisations most likely do not reach it. Not tax advice This needs a look from whoever files for the LLC, ideally before the September cook repeats the same pattern at a larger scale.
Every 1.00 spent returned 2.41 in gross receipts. Cost of goods ran 41.5% of takings. The 443.38 kept offsets 28.9% of the 1,533.61 the Pasadena Livestock Show & Rodeo cook has cost so far.

GKK treats crew time as contributed, not as a cost. These hours are priced at zero everywhere in this report, deliberately. Nothing physical ran out. The constraint was demand. Every input had headroom at close — the tightest was meat and onion at roughly 52 to 57 plates, and even buns would have carried 80. 42 plates sold against 80 buns bought is a 6-bun overage on the 48 opened, which is an independent check on the plate count that does not depend on chips or on Square. The event stopped because the queue did, not because the tent did.
The 10–15 plates of unsold capacity were worth 150.00–225.00, and the goods were already paid for. Selling them would have cost nothing at the margin and, with 14 hours of crew slack already in the day, no extra labour either. Net retained would have run 593.38 to 668.38 instead of 443.38 — a 34 to 51% uplift for no additional spend. This is the largest single lever in the event, and it is a marketing and pre-sale problem, not a purchasing one.
A large share of the 175.00 Restaurant Depot line is inventory, not cost of goods sold. One unopened box of 50 chips, 4 packs of buns, three quarters of a jar each of pickles and jalapenos, most of a half-gallon of sauce, and leftover clamshells all carry forward to the September cook. They are paid for and they are real value on the books. Cost per plate of 8.04 therefore overstates what a plate actually consumed, and the overstatement cannot be quantified until the Restaurant Depot ticket is recovered. Leftover cooked meat is the exception: it was split among the four owners, which is a distribution rather than an asset.
