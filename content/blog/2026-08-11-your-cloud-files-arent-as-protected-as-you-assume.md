Plenty of businesses keep everything in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace and treat that as their data protection strategy. It isn't one.

A cloud platform does an excellent job of keeping your team connected and working. Being connected, though, is not the same thing as being protected. If a file gets deleted, overwritten or locked up by ransomware, your cloud provider has no obligation to bring it back — and wasn't built to. That responsibility sits with you.

The industry term for this is the shared responsibility model. Your provider is accountable for keeping the platform online and the underlying infrastructure secure. Safeguarding the data inside it, and getting that data back, is on your side of the line.

And the ways it goes wrong are far more routine than most business owners expect.

**Five ways cloud data disappears**

Cloud platforms sync quickly, which is exactly what makes them useful. It's also what makes them unforgiving. The moment a file is removed, altered or encrypted, that change propagates to every connected device — usually before anyone notices something is off.

Here's where the losses typically come from:

**1. Someone deletes the wrong thing.** A shared folder gets removed by mistake, and the deletion follows the sync across the cloud and every connected machine. Suddenly your staff can't open files they need to do their jobs. Productivity stalls while the team pieces together what disappeared and whether any of it is recoverable.

**2. A good file gets overwritten.** An employee saves over the current version of an important document without realizing it. Now people are digging through email attachments, download folders and old copies trying to reconstruct the original — and the customer work attached to it slips.

**3. Ransomware rides the sync.** Encryption starts on a single endpoint. If those files sync to the cloud, the encrypted copies sync right along with them. Contracts, project files and customer records become unusable, and without a clean copy from before the infection, recovery gets significantly harder.

**4. The retention window closes.** Cloud platforms only hold onto deleted or modified data for a set period. Discover the problem after that window expires and your options narrow fast. A single missing file becomes a real disruption when it's tied to invoicing, compliance, service delivery or a customer commitment.

**5. Permissions get set wrong.** One misconfigured share can lock your own people out of the files they need — or hand sensitive data to someone who shouldn't see it. Either version costs the team hours and drags managers into a cleanup they never planned for.

Working from anywhere is a genuine advantage. It just doesn't replace a recovery plan. Skip the tested backup strategy and each of these scenarios turns into lost hours, slow customer response and a lot of uncertainty at the exact moment you need straight answers.

**A backup you've never tested isn't a plan**

Most businesses have something in place. That's different from knowing whether what's in place is complete, current and actually restorable. Finding out during an outage, a breach or a data loss event is a bad time to learn the difference.

Routine testing surfaces the problems while they're still cheap to fix — corrupted files, folders that were never included in the job, backups that only look successful in the dashboard. It also puts a documented recovery path in your team's hands ahead of time.

The first time you walk through a restore shouldn't be the day you need one. You should already know what can be recovered, roughly how long it will take and who owns each step of the process.

That knowledge is what keeps employees working, customers answered and managers out of crisis mode.

**Resilience is knowing you can come back**

No business prevents every disruption. Downtime happens. So does ransomware, accidental deletion and the occasional system failure that cuts everyone off from the files they need.

What changes is how you meet it. With the right foundation underneath you, a disruption becomes a process you execute rather than a problem you improvise.

That foundation is where an IT partner earns their keep — verifying your backups are actually complete, testing the restore procedures before they matter and shortening the path back to normal operations when something finally breaks.
