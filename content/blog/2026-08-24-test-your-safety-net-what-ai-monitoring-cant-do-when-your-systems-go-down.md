## Test Your Safety Net: What AI Monitoring Can't Do When Your Systems Go Down

It's 4 a.m. and an alert just fired. Something critical is down.

The monitoring tool caught it fast, exactly like it's supposed to. But the alert isn't the hard part. What happens in the next hour is.

Monitoring is good at spotting trouble — unusual activity, a failed backup, a server that stopped responding, and getting the right person notified fast. What it can't do is restore your data, rebuild the system, or get your crew back to work. That's a different job, and it has to be done before the alert ever fires.

**Detection and recovery are not the same thing**

A fire alarm tells you there's a fire. It doesn't put it out. Whether the building survives depends entirely on what was already in place — extinguishers, a plan, people who know the exits.

Monitoring works the same way. It'll tell you a server's down or a backup failed. It won't tell you how long your team sits idle waiting for direction, or what an hour of downtime costs you on a job with a deadline.

**Your real safety net**

Disruptions aren't a question of if. Two businesses can get hit by the same outage on the same day and land in completely different places — one back online in hours, the other still figuring out what to do three days later.

The difference isn't the tools. It's whether there was already a documented plan: who's responsible, what happens first, and in what order. The businesses that recover fast tested that plan beforehand, found what broke, and fixed it before it mattered. When the real thing hits, they're executing, not improvising.

**How prepared are you? Ask yourself:**

* When did we last test our backups, and did the restore actually work?
* If something failed today, how long would recovery take?
* Does everyone know their role if a critical system goes down?
* Are we confident our backups would work right now, not in theory?
* Could we get the team back to work today, in a timeframe we could live with?
